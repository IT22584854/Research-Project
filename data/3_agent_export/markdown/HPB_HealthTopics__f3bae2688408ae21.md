# Director’s Office
**The Future of the Health Promotion Bureau: A Vision for Excellence**
Sri Lanka stands at a turning point, where health promotion must evolve to meet new challenges. The Health Promotion Bureau has always played a vital role in empowering communities with knowledge and tools to improve their well-being. But today, we must think beyond traditional approaches. We must embrace innovation, technology, and collaboration to build a healthier nation.
Imagine a future where every Sri Lankan has instant access to reliable health information at their fingertips. A future where artificial intelligence, mobile apps, and telemedicine bridge the gap between healthcare professionals and the people who need them most. The digital revolution is here, and we must harness its power to transform the way we promote health. By creating AI-driven chatbots, predictive analytics, and interactive health platforms, we can ensure that knowledge reaches everyone, regardless of their location or background.
But technology alone is not enough. True progress comes when communities take ownership of their health. We must engage with mothers, young people, and grassroots organizations, giving them the tools to become health champions. When young minds use social media, gaming, and storytelling to spread positive health messages, we create a ripple effect that transforms entire communities. When we empower local groups with the resources to design health initiatives that suit their unique needs, we lay the foundation for lasting change.
Health is not just about medical care. It is about the air we breathe, the food we eat, and the environments we live in. Climate change is altering disease patterns, increasing the risk of natural disasters, and threatening food security. We must prepare for these challenges by integrating climate resilience into our health promotion strategies. Through early warning systems, community-based resilience programs, and sustainable practices, we can protect the health of our people today and for generations to come.
Mental health is another area that demands urgent attention. In today’s fast-paced world, stress, anxiety, and depression are affecting more lives than ever before. We must normalize conversations about mental health, integrating it into our education system, workplaces, and healthcare services. By breaking the stigma, expanding support networks, and using digital tools for early intervention, we can ensure that mental well-being becomes a national priority.
As Sri Lanka’s population ages, we must also focus on lifelong health promotion. Our elderly deserve not just longer lives, but healthier and more fulfilling ones. We must support active aging through exercise programs, community support networks, and digital literacy training to keep older adults connected and engaged. A society that respects and values its elders is a society that thrives.
For the Health Promotion Bureau to remain a leader, we must invest in knowledge, training, and research. Our health professionals must be equipped with the latest skills in digital health, risk communication, and behavioral science. We must build partnerships with universities, global organizations, and private sector leaders to drive evidence-based health initiatives. And we must measure our impact, continuously refining our strategies to ensure they are effective.
Trust is the foundation of everything we do. In an age of misinformation, the public needs a reliable source of health knowledge. We must strengthen our credibility by ensuring that every piece of information we share is backed by science. We must be proactive in countering myths and false claims, using technology to rapidly debunk misinformation. And we must engage with the public through open dialogue, listening to their concerns and providing answers they can rely on.
Sustaining this vision requires long-term commitment and funding. Health promotion cannot be an afterthought—it must be woven into national policy, backed by dedicated resources, and supported by strong collaborations. Whether through government funding, private sector partnerships, or international grants, we must secure the financial stability needed to drive meaningful change.
The road ahead is not without challenges, but we have the vision, the expertise, and the determination to overcome them. By embracing innovation, empowering communities, and staying true to our mission, the Health Promotion Bureau can lead Sri Lanka into a healthier future. This is our moment to redefine health promotion, to push boundaries, and to set new standards of excellence. Together, we can build a nation where health is not a privilege, but a fundamental right for all.

Annual Preschool Health Promotion Review
The Preschool Health Promotion Setting is one of the 5 settings focused for health promotion by the Health Promotion Bureau. The progress of preschool health promotion settings development program is reviewed annually by the Planning, Monitoring and Evaluation unit. The key stakeholders representing health, early childhood development and preschool settings from provincial, district and divisional levels gather at the review to present the progress based on three key areas of evaluation: establishment, functionality and multi-sector engagement in implementation of preschool health promotion program. While progress is reviewed, many success stories and experiences are shared for wider learning. This year the annual preschool health promotion progress reviews started from 26th November 2024 where Western and Southern Provinces were reviewed. On 29th November Central, Uva and Sabaragamuwa Provinces, on 03rd December the North Western and North Central Provinces and on 10th December Northern and Eastern Provinces were reviewed. All officials from the different sectors were also appreciated by awarding a certificate of appreciation. UNICEF is the sole funding agency for this program.
