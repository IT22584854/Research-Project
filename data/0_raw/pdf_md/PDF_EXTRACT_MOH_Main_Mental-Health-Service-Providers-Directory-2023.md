## Preface

Mental  health  is  not  merely  the  absence  of  mental  disorders.  The  World  Health  Organization defines mental health as 'a state of well-being in which the individual realizes his or her own abilities, can cope with the normal stresses of life, can work productively and fruitfully, and is able to make a contribution to his or her community' (World Health Report, 2001).

We all have to face day today life stressors. It is an inevitable part of our lives. Most of us are able to manage stress, while some people may have difficulties in coping with stress. There can be situations where they might need psycho-social support to manage their mental health issues. There  are  different  institutions-  state  as  well  as  private,  to  support  people  with  psycho-social issues. Ministry of Health has established a mental health help line - 1926 to facilitate emergency needs of  general  public  in  mental  health  related  conditions.  The  Directorate  of  Mental  Health with World Health Organization (WHO) and National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH) has extended the national helpline -1926 to all districts.

Persons  working  towards  the  improvement  of  mental  wellbeing  need  information  on  mental health  and  psycho-social  service  providers  including  counseling  in  order  to  facilitate  better mental  health.    This  directory  provides  district  wise  information  compiled  according  to  five sections: Government Hospitals, Other Government Institutions, Non-Governmental Organizations,  Children  Homes  &amp;  Care  Services  and  Elders'  Homes  &amp;  Care  Services.  The information  provided  in  this  directory  will  facilitate  users  to  easy  reference  related  to  mental health services in their respective districts.

Publication  of  this  book  has  been  an  achievement  of  a  long-felt  need.  Information  in  this directory will help users' easy references. This directory comprises of the information collected from each district relevant to all aspects  of  mental  health  service  provision.  The  health  sector alone cannot work towards improvement of mental health of people. Many other sectors such as education, social services, youth affairs, child and women's affairs, justice and other government and non-governmental organizations have a significant role. The content of this directory is  a proof that multi-sectoral involvement is already in place in the field of mental health.

## Dr. Rohan Ratnayake Director/Mental Health

## Message from the former Chair Professor, Department of Psychiatry, University of Sri Jayawardenepura

World Health  Organization  estimates  a  significant  proportion  of  the  world  population,  one  in eight live with mental disorders. This demonstrates enormous need for mental health care in the world. Although effective treatment is available, most people do not have access to mental health care facilities. Mental health has not received its due place in many cultures. Children, adults and senior citizens with severe mental disorders are vulnerable and they need specialized services to live a near normal life. Members of the society who are studying, working, bringing up children also affected by mental illnesses. They lose their dignity and quality of life by not being able to access mental health care facilities to recover from the condition.

Sri Lanka has ever increasing demand for mental health services. The reasons for the increasing mental  health  demand  are  multi-faceted.  A  prolonged  armed  conflict  of  the  country,  breaking down of traditional value systems, increasing stress levels, economic hardships, increase of drug use and violence contribute to mental ill-health of masses. Anxiety, depression, post-traumatic disorder and other stress related disorders are on the rise, demanding health care provision closer to their homes to lead productive lives. People with serious mental health concerns are across the life-cycle and they live in the community. To mention a few, developmental delay and autism in children,  addiction  disorders  in  adolescents,  schizophrenia,  severe  bipolar  disorder  in  adults, depressive disorder in mothers and dementia in the elderly are conditions that affect the whole family.  Persons  with  mental  disorders  need  specialized  care  closer  to  their  homes.  In  this backdrop, mapping of available mental resources in the country is a felt need.

The  update  of  the  directory  was  a  collaborative  effort  by  the  Directorate  of    Mental  Health, Ministry  of  Health  and  the  Professorial  Psychiatry  Unit  of  the  Faculty  of  Medical  Sciences, University of Sri Jayawardenepura.

Prof. Samudra T. Kathriarachchi Former chair Professor of Psychiatry University of Sri Jayawardenepura

## Message from the Deputy Director General/ Non-Communicable Diseases

Mental health needs of community are vast and vary according to the ones who recognize the need. A considerable number of persons with mental disorders do not seek professional help, and the  result  is  a  considerable  treatment  gap.  This  publication  developed  by  the  Directorate  of Mental  Health,  Ministry  of  Health,  will  be  helpful  for  the  services  addressing  to  reduce  the unmet mental health needs and improve access to mental health services.

Mental health service providers' Directory is a main comprehensive document of the Directorate of Mental Health that present the details to obtain mental health services throughout the country. This  aims  to  facilitate  the  mental  health  services  in  the  country  and  to  increase  awareness  on available mental health services among health care service providers.

I  would  like  to  extend  my  sincere  gratitude  to  all  the  contributors  for  their  hard  work  in completing  this  publication.  I  hope  that  the  users  of  this  directory  will  be  able  to  provide  a comprehensive care to make this valuable publication more useful.

Dr. Champika Wickramasinghe Deputy Director General/Non-Communicable Diseases
