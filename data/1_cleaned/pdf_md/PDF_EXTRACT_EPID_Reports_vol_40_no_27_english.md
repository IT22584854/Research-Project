## Diagnosis of Measles

As mentioned in the previous article, number of measles  cases  has  shown  an  increase.  Following  article describes different methods available for diagnosis of measles  and  advantages  and  disadvantages  of  each method.

## Measles virus belongs to the

- Sub family - Paramyxovirinae
- Family - Paramyxoviridae
- Genus - Morbilivirus

Measles  is  a  Monotypic  Virus  (i.e.  has  only  a  single serotype) and it has 8 classes (A to H) &amp; 20 genotypes (e.g.  A,  B1-3,  C1-2,  D1-8,  E,  F,  G1-2,  H1-2).  Some  of these types are localised in specific regions (Endemic) and some are inactive.

## PCR

Genotypic characterization

## Samples for virus isolation

Samples for virus isolation should be collected within 5  days of the onset of rash. Though virus isolation is useful for genetic information, it is not useful for diagnosis  of  measles.  Samples  should  be  collected  at  the same time as the IgM sample (Virus isolation is positive in 40% of the samples which are  positive for measles  IgM) and its virus isolation can be done using

- Naso-Pharyngeal  Aspirate  (NPA)  or  nasal  / throat swab in VTM
- Peripheral  Blood  Lymphocytes
- Urine:10-50  ml  centrifuged  &amp;  pellet  reconstituted in  VTM

## Naso-pharyngeal aspirates

<!-- image -->

- Need  trained  medical  personnel
- good  specimen  for  rubella &amp; measles
- Need Special equipment

## Urine Sediments

- contamination can be a problem
- Easy  to  obtain  except  from very young children
- Need  centrifugation processing
- Not  a  good  specimen  for rubella virus

## Throat Swabs / nasal swabs

- Very important to collect within 0-5 days after the onset of rash / first contact with the case
- Good specimens for both measles and rubella
- Should be collected and transported properly

## Blood Lymphocytes

Difficult to isolate Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cells (PBMCs) from blood

Not a good specimen for rubella

Virus Isolation is done using Cell lines-

- PMK cells
- Vero/hSLAM cells
- B95a cells

CPEgiant cells with multiple nuclei, syncitia formation, Positive in 3-5 days

## Confirmation

- Immuno fluorescence
- RT PCR &amp; Sequencing

## Samples for serology

Venous Blood - 3 ml of blood should be collected by venepuncture

- Sample should be collected into a clean, dry, screw capped bottle without anticoagulant
- Skin should be cleaned with 70% alcohol
- Label and leave at room temperature for 30 minutes
- Serum should be separated if transport takes several days
- Store &amp; transport at 4 0 C

Note-Haemolysed  (wet  containers  cause  haemolysis  of  blood)  or  contaminated blood is not suitable

Serological diagnosis of measles dependent on the time of specimen collection

- first 72 hours - 77%  of 1 st  samples IgM positive
- 28 days - 90% of all 2 nd  samples IgM  positive
- 4 - 11 days - 100% 1 st  samples IgM positive

For unvaccinated persons - 94%

Transport of specimens

Specimens should always be sent with a request form and the form should contain

- Patient details
- Relevant history

Labelling of specimens

- Proper labelling is important and the label should contain
- Test required

Transport in a safe container

When transporting specimen to distant laboratories

- Pack in a 2 nd  container
- Specimen  container  should  be  packed  in  a  box  with  absorbent cotton wool (enough to absorb contents)
- Transport in vaccine carrier or box with ice packs around it.
- Special  details  (e.g.  sample  from  a  HIV  or  HBsAg  positive  patient)  should  be  indicated  in  request  form  and  on  the  outside container

## Oral fluid

- Good patient (and guardian) acceptance
- Easy to collect
- Non Invasive

Stability of oral fluid

- Stable for IgM - 7 days 37°C and 42°C (IgM capture assay, HPA)
- Oral Fluid Samples have been successfully used for surveillance of measles (and Rubella and Mumps) in the UK for &gt; 12 years
- Stable for PCR - 7 days 37°C and 42°C

## Oral Fluid sampling: Challenges

- Supplies of Oral fluid collection devices usually need to be supplied to health centres
- Easy to use but training for collection and testing needed
- No  easy  mechanism  for  determining  adequacy  of  samples  collected
- Quality Assurance programme is yet to be fully established Can use confirmatory testing as for serum PT yet to be developed
- Must use MicroImmune assay

## Filter paper samples

- Spot 30ul of serum in the middle of each circle (Add 4x 30ul of serum from the same patient on one card)
- Vortex serum for 10s
- Label the filtercard with serum ID
- After minimum 1h: Put the filter cards in a zip-lock bag preferably with Silica gel desiccant
- Dry samples for at least 1 h in a Filter card holder
- Once the samples are dry, the samples can be stored at 4 0 C
- Put all filter cards (in zip-lock bags) in an envelope and send to RRL  by  conventional  mail  service  (Samples  can  be  shipped  at room temperature)

The  assay  recommended  by  WHO  for  serum  is  ELISA  for  IgM  (Sri Lanka is currently using this method for diagnosing measles)

- One serum sample collected between     3-28 days - collected at first contact
- High positive predictive value
- Highly sensitive &amp; specific
- Easy to perform
- Good commercial kits are available
- Quick accurate results
